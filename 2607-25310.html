Paper overview

This paper studies PFM-1 landmine detection in a UAV visible and near-infrared hyperspectral scene, asking not only whether target and background pixels can be separated but also how many spatial candidates must be inspected before each physical target region is found. That operational perspective matters in rare-target screening, where an apparently excellent global ranking can still leave the final target buried beneath a long tail of false alarms.

The experiment compares centered and uncentered spectral angle mapper variants, matched filter, adaptive coherence estimator, and constrained energy minimization under three signature sources: a ground-measured SVC spectrum, a fully informed in-scene core signature, and a retrospective bootstrap. In the bootstrap, spatial proposals are checked against ground truth; verified regions contribute central pixels to a cumulative signature; and the detector is rerun. The paper combines ROC-AUC and average precision with discovery curves, per-target candidate milestones, and spatial score maps.

On the single scene containing seven visible target regions, adaptive coherence estimation reaches the last discovery milestone after nine candidate proposals, while matched filtering and constrained energy minimization require more and both spectral-angle variants have very long discovery tails. These values are informative descriptions of this released pipeline, not established detector rankings: the endpoint reproduces the fully informed signature by construction, and implementation issues make several exact comparisons provisional.

Key strengths

Physical-target accounting reveals what pixel ROC-AUC hides. Reporting target-discovery trajectories and candidate-review milestones makes the rare-event burden visible. This is the paper's clearest, application-specific contribution and a useful evaluation pattern beyond this particular detector study.

The evidence presentation is complementary and unusually inspectable. Average precision, precision-recall behavior, discovery curves, target milestones, and spatial maps expose detector behavior from several angles instead of relying on one saturated global metric.

The controlled comparison is compact and conceptually clear. The scene, detector families, and background statistics are held fixed while signature source changes, and the procedural equations make the retrospective state transition easy to follow.

The contribution boundary is appropriately restrained. The authors do not present a new detector or adaptation theory and explicitly describe the verifier as retrospective. That discipline keeps attention on the evaluation and accounting contribution.

The public implementation provides real audit value. The repository includes the five detector variants, data instructions, the final-bootstrap execution path, event outputs, and committed summaries. Although incomplete as an end-to-end reproduction package, it is substantive enough to expose important implementation details.

Key concerns and constructive suggestions

The causal benefit of signature updating is not isolated. Reviewed-area blocking and continued exploration change alongside the signature, there is no identical-policy fixed-signature trajectory, the terminal match is guaranteed by construction, and final metrics reuse the targets that formed the signature. To address this, run matched update-on and update-off trajectories under identical proposal, blocking, batching, tie, stopping, and budget rules, then evaluate each target while excluding it from signature construction.

The simulated human feedback is an exact label-and-mask oracle. A successful proposal immediately supplies the correct region identity and complete target mask, with no false confirmations, missed targets, localization error, or ambiguity. To address this, define how point, box, coarse-mask, or binary feedback becomes target pixels and replay the full adaptive loop under measured or systematically varied label and localization errors.

The exact detector comparisons rest on an unstable computational chain. The released constrained energy minimization path uses inconsistent coordinate systems, equal-score handling can affect metrics and proposal order, verification and blocking use different geometries, and the terminal batch continues after the reported last-discovery milestone. To address this, correct the formulas, unify geometry, define deterministic tie rules, stop immediately at completion, and regenerate every table, figure, score map, and event trace from one pinned driver.

One scene and seven visible targets cannot establish stable rankings or general behavior. Neighboring pixels are not independent replications, and the study reports no scene- or target-level uncertainty for its main comparisons. To address this, repeat the matched protocol across independent sites or flight lines and target conditions, using paired scene-level analysis with target clustering and interval estimates.

The signature estimator and adaptive path are not stress-tested. The central-half heuristic, pixel-weighted averaging, spatial-policy parameters, and feedback path are each fixed without sensitivity analysis; uncentered spectral angle mapping also suffers a large average-precision decline after refinement. To address this, compare core fractions, full masks, erosion, robust and region-balanced aggregation, weak-region estimators, and policy parameters while tracing detector-specific rank changes round by round.

Candidate count is only a proxy for operational burden, and completion uses oracle knowledge. The study does not connect proposal counts to unique reviewed area, analyst time, error, travel, cost, or safety, and deployment would not know when all targets had been found. To address this, predeclare budgeted target recall and a stopping rule based on observable scores or patience, then validate site count against reviewed area, elapsed time, and human error.

What would most improve this paper

1. Correct and determinize the complete result chain. Canonicalize constrained energy minimization, use tie-aware metrics and stable proposal ordering, unify inspection geometry, separate discovery milestones from processed reviews, and regenerate all central results from one pinned command.

2. Isolate updating with a matched held-out experiment. Compare update-on and update-off under the same review policy and budget, evaluating only targets that did not construct the current signature.

3. Replace exact-mask feedback with a realistic supervision ladder. Test point, box, coarse-mask, noisy-mask, missed-confirmation, and false-confirmation conditions, paired with a deployable budget, threshold, or patience stopping rule.

4. Add independent replication and appropriate uncertainty. Evaluate multiple scenes, flight lines, backgrounds, and target states, treating the scene or physical target cluster rather than pixels as the unit of inference.

5. Validate the estimator and publish full provenance. Include the existing full-mask path, core-fraction and weighting sweeps, robust alternatives, detector-specific failure analysis, saved score maps and event traces, environment locking, and regression tests.
