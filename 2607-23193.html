OmniScope: Modality-Decoupled Token Compression for Omnimodal Large Language Models

Jinsen Su, Yongdong Luo, Yuexiao Ma, Yibo Hu et al. · July 2026 · arXiv:2607.23193

OmniScope offers a coherent training-free design and substantial measured prefill savings by estimating audio and visual relevance independently. The key next step is an audit-ready, paper-exact evaluation with paired uncertainty and reference-faithful baselines.

Paper overview

OmniScope addresses the inference cost of long audio-video inputs in omnimodal large language models. Its motivating observation is that answer-relevant moments in audio and video need not coincide, so using one modality to decide what survives in the other can discard unique evidence.

The framework uses the query as a shared semantic anchor while estimating relevance separately for audio and vision. It assigns modality-specific budgets, applies anchor-delta spatiotemporal compression to preserve visual context and change, and merges audio tokens within each second to reduce redundancy. The entire pipeline is training-free and wraps an existing Qwen2.5-Omni model rather than changing its weights.

Across WorldSense, DailyOmni, OmniVideoBench, and Video-MME, the paper reports the strongest average accuracy among its compressed comparators at 45% and 25% token retention for both 3B and 7B backbones. The clearest systems result is on 7B WorldSense: at 25% retention, prefill time falls from 6299.90 ms to 1784.45 ms and peak memory from 28.31 GB to 24.00 GB, while average accuracy falls by 0.35 points. These efficiency results are meaningful, although the accuracy ordering and broader scalability claims need stronger controls.

Key strengths

A well-motivated modality-decoupled composition. The method directly targets cross-modal salience mismatch by letting audio and vision earn separate token budgets from the same query. Prior work contains the individual scoring, clustering, differencing, and merging ingredients, but the review found no exact predecessor for this independently query-conditioned, two-modality training-free composition.

Substantial measured prefill savings. The 7B WorldSense results show that aggressive token reduction can materially reduce the expensive prefill stage and lower peak memory. Reporting prefill and end-to-end latency separately is especially useful because it exposes the cost of external CLIP preprocessing instead of hiding it behind token-count claims.

Low-friction integration. OmniScope requires no additional training data, optimization, or changes to the base model weights. That makes the approach potentially useful when retraining is unavailable or costly, provided the extra encoder, preprocessing, and integration requirements are fully documented.

Broad evaluation within one model family. Four audio-video benchmarks, two backbone sizes, and two aggressive retention settings reduce the risk that the observed behavior is tied to a single dataset or model scale. The paper also reports both quality and systems measurements rather than relying only on theoretical complexity.

A modular and inspectable pipeline. Separate allocation, visual compression, and audio merging stages support targeted ablations and make the design easier to analyze. The existing component studies provide a useful starting point for stronger causal and interaction tests.

Key concerns and constructive suggestions

The small comparative accuracy lead has no uncertainty estimate. OmniScope's average advantage over the strongest compressed comparator is only 0.375–0.50 points, yet the paper reports no item-level intervals, paired tests, run counts, or variance. The observed ordering could reflect evaluation noise rather than a reliable method advantage. To address this, release item-level predictions and report paired bootstrap intervals or suitable paired tests for every dataset and aggregate, with repeated decoding when nondeterminism remains.

The released allocator is not the algorithm described in the manuscript. Public code adds cubic visual-score and squared audio-score transforms, an audio self-boost, minimum token floors, and rectangular count constraints that can materially change which evidence survives. This breaks attribution between the paper's stated method and its reported results. To address this, provide explicit paper_exact and reported_results modes, expose every transform and floor in immutable configuration, and run paired component ablations across all reported settings.

Baseline fidelity and public auditability are incomplete. The adapted FastV changes both its attention signal and modality scope without validating equivalence to the reference method, while the repository omits executable paths for three datasets, baseline methods, metric scoring, and efficiency measurement. Sub-point comparisons are difficult to interpret under these conditions. To address this, validate FastV first in its original setting and release pinned baseline commits, per-table configs, scoring scripts, timing harnesses, raw outputs, and expected results.

The core salience and decoupling hypothesis lacks causal validation. CLIP and audio-query similarities make irreversible retention decisions, but aggregate downstream accuracy does not show whether answer-critical evidence was preserved or whether independent allocation is specifically responsible for the gains. To address this, measure evidence recall and oracle gaps on aligned, conflicting, audio-only, visual-only, silent, and rapid-change subsets, comparing independent, directional, joint, uniform, and random allocation under identical budgets.

Long-context and cross-model claims exceed the tested scope. Evaluation caps videos at 128 frames, systems measurements are confined to WorldSense, and both backbones belong to Qwen2.5-Omni. The 3B model also loses 1.50 accuracy points at 25% retention, and its 45% end-to-end latency is slower than full input. To address this, sweep input and generation lengths to practical context limits, report preprocessing, prefill, decode, memory, throughput, and energy separately, and test an architecturally distinct OmniLLM.

The novelty boundary needs sharper positioning. Query-guided visual compression, clustering, temporal differencing, and token merging are established primitives, while QG-VTC and FocusLLaVA are absent from the rendered related-work discussion. To address this, restore the missing comparisons and frame the contribution specifically as the independently query-conditioned omnimodal composition, supported by a component-level prior-work table.

What would most improve this paper

1. Establish a canonical executable method. Reconcile the paper and code through explicit paper-exact and result-reproduction modes, typed configuration, zero-score fallbacks, budget invariants, and paired ablations of every undocumented allocation choice.

2. Release the complete experiment and baseline stack. Make every main-table cell reproducible from a documented command with pinned models, datasets, baselines, metrics, system settings, exact token counts, and raw outputs; include a reference-fidelity check for FastV.

3. Re-evaluate the headline comparisons with paired inference. Publish item-level results, confidence intervals, and repeated-run details where needed, then calibrate claims to the measured uncertainty and report the full range of behavior across model sizes.

4. Test the decoupling mechanism directly. Build controlled alignment and conflict subsets, annotate answer evidence, compare coupled and independent allocations at matched budgets, and report evidence retention after each compression stage.

5. Bound scalability and deployment claims. Measure quality and full-system costs across longer audio-video inputs, different generation lengths, both tested model sizes, and another OmniLLM family so that overhead break-even points and failure regimes are explicit.
